ChatGPT Is More Confident Than You, Even When It's Completely Wrong

A lawyer asked ChatGPT whether the court cases it gave him were real. It said yes. None of them existed.

Steven Schwartz was a lawyer with 30 years of experience. He used ChatGPT to help him with legal research. ChatGPT gave him six court cases, including case names, judges, dates, and quotes. Everything looked correct.

But something made him suspicious. So, he asked ChatGPT directly: Are these cases real?

It said yes. This is the part many people miss. He actually checked. He asked the AI, and it confirmed that the information it had made up was real.

All six cases were fake. Judge Kevin Castel later described the fake court decision as "gibberish." Schwartz, his colleague Peter LoDuca, and their law firm were ordered to pay a total fine of $5,000. This happened mainly because they continued to support the fake cases even after the court questioned whether they were real.

The story was reported in newspapers around the world.

The case names didn't exist. The judges didn't exist. The quotes had never been said by anyone.

And the AI never showed any sign that it was unsure.

If you have ever copied an AI answer and used it for something important, this article is for you.

What Is This Called?

When AI gives you information that sounds confident, looks detailed, but is completely wrong, it is called an AI hallucination.

The word might sound strange. AI doesn't actually see things. It creates text.

The meaning is simple:

AI gives you information with confidence, even when that information is completely made up.

And here is what makes it dangerous. An AI hallucination doesn't look like a mistake.

There is no warning. No sign that something is wrong. No question mark. It looks just like a correct answer and sounds just as confident.

When a person doesn't know something, they usually say, "I'm not sure."

But when AI doesn't know something, it can give you a very detailed answer that is completely wrong.

Why Does It Happen?

You can understand this in a few simple lines.

An AI like ChatGPT is not a database. It doesn't automatically search for and check every answer. It predicts the next word based on patterns it has learned from a huge amount of text.

Most of the time, this works very well.

But when AI gets a question and doesn't have enough information to answer correctly, it doesn't always stop.

It keeps generating answers.

The problem is that a sentence that sounds correct is not always true.

So, AI creates an answer that looks real and gives it with the same confidence as a correct answer.

It isn't exactly lying. Lying means knowing the truth but choosing to say something false.

AI doesn't necessarily know which information is true or false. It simply keeps generating an answer based on what it has learned.

Three Words That Can Tell You AI Is Guessing

This is one of the most useful things in this article. Learning to notice these words can help you identify answers that need extra checking.

When AI doesn't have enough information, it sometimes uses general words that make its answer sound correct:

"Typically..."

"Usually..."

"Generally speaking..."

Let's understand with a real-life example.

Imagine asking AI whether your company's insurance covers dental treatment.

It might answer:

"Yes, dental treatments are typically covered under standard medical insurance, usually up to 80%."

Read that again. It sounds like an answer about your insurance policy.

But it isn't.

It is only describing what insurance policies might generally cover. AI has no idea what your actual policy says.

It has filled the missing information with an answer that sounds correct.

These three words don't always mean AI is wrong, but they are a good reason to check the answer.

Where Can AI Hallucinations Actually Cause Problems?

Not every AI mistake is important. Let's understand which mistakes can cause serious problems.

Low risk: Generating ideas, rewriting your text, or explaining something you already understand. You can easily notice mistakes, and usually nothing serious happens.

High risk: Names, numbers, dates, prices, quotes, references, and anything related to law, health, or money.

The rule is simple: The more specific and important the information is, the more carefully you should check it.

Let's look at another real example.

In February 2023, Google shared a demonstration of its AI chatbot called Bard.

During the demonstration, Bard gave a wrong answer about the James Webb Space Telescope. It said that the telescope had taken the first pictures of a planet outside our solar system.

But that wasn't true. Those pictures were actually taken in 2004 by a different telescope.

The mistake was noticed within a few hours. Google's parent company lost around $100 billion in market value the next day.

One wrong answer. One day.

Five Things You Should Always Check

You don't need to check every single sentence AI gives you. You just need to build a habit of checking the five things that can cause the biggest problems.

1. Open Every Link

If AI gives you a website or a source, open it yourself.

It only takes a few seconds and helps you find links or sources that don't actually exist.

2. Search for Every Important Quote

If you want to use a quote from a person, book, or research paper, find the original source.

Never blindly trust a quote provided by AI.

3. Check Every Important Number

If you are preparing a report, presentation, or making an important decision, always check the numbers using a source you have opened yourself.

4. Ask, "Does This Actually Exist?"

If you are not sure about something, ask AI directly:

"Does this actually exist, or are you creating an answer that only sounds real?"

Pay attention to its response, but remember that AI can still give you the wrong confirmation.

5. Ask It to Show the Source

Ask AI:

"Where did you get this information? Can you provide the original source?"

Then search for that source yourself.

If you cannot find it, don't trust the information until you can verify it.

The Habit That Can Solve Most of This Problem

If you remember only one thing from this article, remember this:

Treat every AI answer as a first draft from a fast assistant who can make mistakes and never has to take responsibility for them.

This simple way of thinking changes how you use AI.

You don't need to reject every answer. You don't need to write everything yourself.

Just check the important information, keep what is correct, and use the final version only after you are confident about the facts.

People who get the most value from AI are not the ones who trust it completely. They are also not the ones who avoid using it.

They are the people who use AI to help with writing and other tasks while keeping responsibility for checking the facts.

One Simple Trick to Reduce Most of the Risk

If you want to change just one habit, start with this.

Give AI the original information and tell it to use only that information.

For example, use this instruction:

"Answer only using the text below. If the answer is not available in the text, say 'not in the document.' Do not use general knowledge."

This instruction helps AI focus on the information you provide instead of using its general knowledge to fill in missing details.

It is also the basic idea behind tools that let you ask questions about your own documents.

After getting an answer, ask:

"Which part of the text did that answer come from?"

If AI cannot show you the exact part, the answer may not be supported by the document.

This method can reduce mistakes, but it cannot completely prevent them.

Conclusion

An AI hallucination happens when AI makes up information instead of saying, "I don't know."

It happens because AI predicts what words should come next, and something that sounds correct is not always true.

This can result in fake court cases, made-up quotes, incorrect numbers, and answers that look perfect but are completely wrong.

AI isn't broken because it makes mistakes. It works by predicting what comes next. Your job is to remember that this doesn't always mean the answer is true.
